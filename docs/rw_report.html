Absorbing random walk: benchmark

How well the experimental direction-respecting walk (--mode walk) finds the faulty enzyme in inborn errors of metabolism, compared with the current-flow method it would replace.

  • Sources are the significantly decreased metabolites and targets the significantly increased ones (decision D20). Current flow gives the same ranks either way; the walk does not.
  • Across 159 patients, the walk puts the causal gene in the top 10 for 6% of them, against 5% for current flow. Median rank: 145 for current flow, 459 for the walk, of 2,302 genes.
  • The walk ranks the causal gene higher than current flow for 28% of patients and in 4 of 20 disorders. Largest group-level gains: GABA-transaminase deficiency 282 → 18, Propionic aciduria 1,187 → 170, Adenylosuccinate lyase deficiency 362 → 71, Methylmalonic aciduria 439 → 106.
  • Largest group-level losses: TMLHE deficiency (4 → 1,172), Thymidine phosphorylase deficiency (1 → 212), 3-Methylcrotonyl-CoA carboxylase deficiency (23 → 1,649) (see where the walk loses).
  • With the other orientation (sources = increased, the protocol's §1.3) the same walk had a group median of 48 and put 28% of patients' causal genes in the top 10 (see orientation).
  • Partial absorption (hook 2, κ) moves the group median by at most 1 rank. Full absorption stays the default.

What was compared

The test. Untargeted plasma metabolomics from patients with a confirmed inborn error (Thistlethwaite et al. 2020, Miller et al. 2015). Each run goes from the patient's metabolite changes to a ranked gene list. The score is where the known causal gene lands among the 2,302 genes on network reactions (1 = top). Group level pools each disorder's patients (BH q < 0.05 and |mean z| ≥ 1); patient level runs each patient alone (|z| ≥ 2).

Current flow is the method used in the sealed blind predictions: Kirchhoff current between every significantly decreased metabolite (source) and every significantly increased one (target) on the undirected network, raw scoring; on an undirected network the orientation doesn't change it. Absorbing walk: walkers start at the decreased metabolites in proportion to |log2FC|, follow reaction directions, and stop at the increased ones. Reversible reactions are split in two, a walker can't undo its last step, dead ends leak, and only walks that reach a target are scored (decision D17). Neighbour baseline ranks each enzyme by how many significant metabolites its own reactions touch.

Same network (3,958 metabolites, 6,377 reactions), same mapping and significance rules for every method; only the scoring step differs. 1 patient with no reachable source–target pair in the walk is left out of the head-to-head patient numbers.

Partial absorption (hook 2)

With κ set, a target (increased metabolite) absorbs a walker with probability 1 − exp(−κ·|log2FC|) and lets the rest walk on to targets further along. On this benchmark it barely moves anything, so the simpler full absorption stays the default.

Direction against the block. In an enzyme deficiency the substrate side usually rises and the product side falls. With sources = decreased metabolites, walkers start on the product side and must follow reaction directions to the increased ones, which lie upstream of the block, so the faulty enzyme is seldom on a successful route. Current flow ignores direction, which is why its ranks do not depend on the orientation.

One-sided disorders. 4 disorders have significant changes in one direction only (Phenylketonuria (186 → 1,134), GABA-transaminase deficiency (282 → 18), HMG-CoA lyase deficiency (55 → 1,001), Holocarboxylase synthetase deficiency (717 → 284)). There the measured, unchanged metabolites stand in for the missing side.

Not scored. No walker reaches a target in Isovaleric aciduria, so the walk gives no ranking there.

These are readings of the runs, not a general test. Across the 19 scored disorders, the share of walkers that reach a target doesn't predict the gain or loss well (correlation of log shares +0.09).

Orientation: which metabolites the walkers start from

Current flow is symmetric: swapping sources and targets only reverses the current, so its ranks are identical under both orientations (checked on every disorder and patient). The walk follows reaction directions, so the orientation matters. Same network, data and settings; only the role of the significant metabolites differs.

Caveats

  • One benchmark, 20 disorders, 159 patients, all plasma. The settings were not tuned on it, but the walk was designed after seeing current flow's results on the same data, so treat any difference as provisional.
  • The walk depends on Human-GEM's reversibility labels (from flux bounds). Wrong labels block or open routes.
  • Leave-one-out scoring isn't defined for the walk; all comparisons here use raw scoring for both methods.
  • The sealed blind predictions were made with current flow. Whether the walk becomes the default should be decided before the next blind round, not after looking at those answers.
