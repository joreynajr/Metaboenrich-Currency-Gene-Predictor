Absorbing random walk: benchmark

How well the experimental direction-respecting walk (--mode walk) finds the faulty enzyme in inborn errors of metabolism under each orientation, compared with the current-flow method. Group level only.

  • The walk is run under two orientations. Sources = up: the significantly increased metabolites are the sources and the decreased ones the targets. Sources = down: the significantly decreased metabolites are the sources and the increased ones the targets (the package default since decision D20).
  • Current flow gives identical ranks under both orientations (checked for all 21 disorders). Median rank 88.5 of 2,302 genes; top 10 for 3 of 20 primary disorders, top 100 for 11.
  • Walk, sources = up: median 48. Top 10 for 5 of 20, top 100 for 13. It ranks the causal gene above current flow in 13 of 20 disorders and below it in 6.
  • Walk, sources = down: median 284 over the 19 it can score; Isovaleric aciduria not scorable. Top 10 for 0 of 20, top 100 for 6. It ranks the causal gene above current flow in 7 of 20 disorders and below it in 12.
  • Sources = up ranks the causal gene higher than sources = down in 14 of 20 disorders. The median share of walkers that reach a target is 6.6% with sources = up and 1.8% with sources = down.
  • Partial absorption (hook 2, κ) moves the group median by at most 2.5 ranks with sources = up and 1 rank with sources = down. Full absorption stays the default.
  • Group level only: each disorder's patients are pooled. Patient-level runs were dropped (decision D21).

What was compared

The test. Untargeted plasma metabolomics from patients with a confirmed inborn error (Thistlethwaite et al. 2020, Miller et al. 2015). Each disorder's patients are pooled (BH q < 0.05 and |mean z| ≥ 1) and the run goes from the pooled metabolite changes to a ranked gene list. The score is where the known causal gene lands among the 2,302 genes on network reactions (1 = top). 20 primary disorders count; 1 secondary case is shown apart.

Current flow is the method used in the sealed blind predictions: Kirchhoff current between every source and every target on the undirected network, raw scoring. Swapping sources and targets only reverses the current, so its ranks are the same under both orientations.

Absorbing walk: walkers start at the sources in proportion to |log2FC|, follow reaction directions, and stop at the targets. Reversible reactions are split in two, a walker can't undo its last step, dead ends leak, and only walks that reach a target are scored (decision D17). Because it follows directions, the orientation matters:

  • Sources = up: significantly increased metabolites are the sources, decreased are the targets (--sources increased; protocol §1.3; the orientation used before D20). Runs in results/bench_rw_up/.
  • Sources = down: significantly decreased metabolites are the sources, increased are the targets (--sources decreased; the package default since D20). Runs in results/bench_rw/.

Neighbour baseline ranks each enzyme by how many significant metabolites its own reactions touch.

Same network (3,958 metabolites, 6,377 reactions), same mapping and significance rules for every method; only the scoring step and the orientation differ.

Partial absorption (hook 2)

With κ set, a target absorbs a walker with probability 1 − exp(−κ·|log2FC|) and lets the rest walk on to targets further along. Under either orientation it barely moves anything, so the simpler full absorption stays the default.

Substrate and product. In an enzyme defect the substrate side usually rises and the product side falls. With sources = up, walkers start at the accumulated substrates and the blocked enzyme lies on their way to the depleted products. With sources = down they start on the product side and must follow reaction directions to the increased metabolites upstream of the block, so the faulty enzyme is seldom on a successful route. In Maple syrup urine disease, for example, the causal gene is ranked 1 with sources = up and 888 with sources = down; current flow ranks it 33 either way. Current flow ignores direction, which is why its ranks do not depend on the orientation.

One-sided disorders. 4 disorders have significant increases only: Phenylketonuria (current flow 186, walk 16 up / 1,134 down), GABA-transaminase deficiency (current flow 282, walk 237 up / 18 down), HMG-CoA lyase deficiency (current flow 55, walk 18 up / 1,001 down), Holocarboxylase synthetase deficiency (current flow 717, walk 320 up / 284 down). With sources = up there are then only sources and the ground absorbs; with sources = down there are only targets and the ground releases the walkers. The measured, unchanged metabolites stand in for the missing side.

These are readings of the runs, not a test.

Caveats

  • One benchmark, 20 disorders, all plasma. The settings were not tuned on it, but the walk was designed after seeing current flow's results on the same data, and both orientations were compared on it, so treat any difference as provisional.
  • Patient-level runs were dropped (decision D21): one patient's z-scores against a reference population are not a differential analysis.
  • The walk depends on Human-GEM's reversibility labels (from flux bounds). Wrong labels block or open routes.
  • Leave-one-out scoring isn't defined for the walk; all comparisons here use raw scoring for every method.
  • The sealed blind predictions were made with current flow. Whether the walk becomes the default, and under which orientation, should be decided before the next blind round, not after looking at those answers.
