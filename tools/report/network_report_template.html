How to read this

Node colour is the metabolite's mean z-score across the disorder's patients: blue decreased, red increased, grey near zero, clipped at |z| = 4. The benchmark data are already standardised, so these are mean z-scores, not log2 fold changes. A small hollow circle with a dashed outline marks a metabolite that was not measured (or could not be mapped): an intermediate on the network with no value.

Dark ring: a significant metabolite (BH q < 0.05 and |mean z| ≥ 1). Increased ones are the sources of current, decreased ones the targets. When a disorder has only increases (or only decreases), every other measured metabolite on the network becomes a ground: current drains to it.

Edge width is current, on one absolute scale for both methods and every view, drawn at a fixed size on screen (zooming does not change it, so the legend always applies). Current flow: Kirchhoff current when one unit is pushed from a source to a target; mean averages over all source–target pairs, max takes the largest pair. It ignores reaction direction. Absorbing walk: net walker traffic across the edge per walker released (mean over sources, or the largest source). It follows reaction direction, and directed cycles can push it above 1.

Arrows show the reaction's direction in the model (substrate → reaction → product); no arrowhead means the reaction is reversible. Squares are reactions, labelled by gene symbols (the reaction id when no gene is known). A violet ring and bold label mark reactions of the disorder's causal gene. Labels are drawn at a fixed size; where two would collide, the less important one is hidden (causal, significant and top-ranked nodes are labelled first) and more appear as you zoom in. Hover any node for its name.

Pathway views come from an over-representation test: which Human-GEM subsystems are enriched among each method's top 100 reactions (hypergeometric test, BH-adjusted; each reaction counts in its first subsystem). Each method contributes its top 6 subsystems with q < 0.05 (or its top 3 by p, flagged not significant, if fewer qualify), and the menu lists the union with each picking method's p and q. A subsystem with more than 150 reactions on the network shows only the reactions that carry current under either method. The causal-gene neighbourhood view shows the causal gene's reactions, their metabolites and the neighbouring reactions that carry current, so the causal gene is always on a map.

Ranks. A reaction's rank is its place among all reactions that carry current under that method (1 = best, raw scoring). The causal-gene rank in the summary is the benchmark's: the gene's place among all genes on the network. Both are recomputed here and checked against the benchmark results on every build.

Mapping caveat. Values sit on the network node the benchmark maps them to. In the Thistlethwaite data the ornithine row carries HMDB03374 (D-ornithine), so in the urea-cycle disorders (OTC deficiency, citrullinemia type I, argininosuccinic aciduria, argininemia) its value is drawn on the D-ornithine node and L-ornithine, next to OTC and ARG1, appears as unmeasured.
